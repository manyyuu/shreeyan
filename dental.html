PATIENT EDUCATION · DENTAL CARE NOTES

Healthy habits.
Clearer dental facts.

Simple explanations for common dental questions. Open a topic to read the key information, practical care tips and a quick myth-versus-fact note.

GUM HEALTH01

Bleeding gums: is it normal?

Healthy gums generally do not bleed with gentle brushing. Bleeding commonly signals gum inflammation from plaque, although other causes can contribute.

Read the key points

Gingivitis can often be improved with effective plaque control and professional care. Periodontitis can affect the tissues and bone supporting teeth.

Practical: Brush gently twice daily with fluoride toothpaste and clean between teeth. Arrange a dental examination if bleeding continues, gums swell or bad breath persists.

MYTH: “Bleeding means I should avoid brushing there.” FACT: gentle, effective cleaning helps control plaque-related inflammation.

PROFESSIONAL CLEANING02

Scaling and polishing: does it loosen teeth?

Scaling removes hardened deposits and plaque that home brushing cannot remove. Existing spaces or gum recession may become more noticeable afterwards.

Read the key points

After deposits are removed, pre-existing spaces, recession or tooth mobility may be easier to notice. This can create the impression that cleaning caused the change.

Practical: Follow the cleaning interval advised after an examination and maintain daily brushing and interdental cleaning. Advanced gum disease may need more than routine polishing.

MYTH: “Scaling creates gaps between teeth.” FACT: it removes deposits that may have been occupying spaces; gum disease may already have caused tissue changes.

DAILY ORAL HYGIENE03

The two-minute brushing routine

Brushing twice daily with fluoride toothpaste helps remove plaque and reduce the risk of tooth decay. A soft-bristled brush and gentle technique matter.

Read the key points

Clean the outer, inner and chewing surfaces, as well as the gumline. Brushing harder does not mean cleaning better and can contribute to abrasion or gum recession.

Practical: Brush for about two minutes twice a day, replace a frayed brush and avoid forceful scrubbing. Follow age-appropriate toothpaste advice for children.

MYTH: “Hard brushing removes more stains.” FACT: technique and fluoride matter more than force.

BETWEEN THE TEETH04

Floss or interdental brush?

A toothbrush does not reliably clean every contact area between teeth. The right interdental tool depends on the spaces between your teeth and your dexterity.

Read the key points

Floss may suit tight contacts. Correctly sized interdental brushes may work better in open spaces, periodontal pockets or around some restorations.

Practical: Ask your dentist to demonstrate the correct size and technique. Clean between your teeth daily, but do not force a brush into a tight space or snap floss into the gums.

MYTH: “Flossing is only for food stuck between teeth.” FACT: interdental cleaning removes plaque from areas a toothbrush may miss.

TOOTH DECAY05

Why frequent sugary snacks matter

Plaque bacteria use free sugars and produce acids that demineralise tooth surfaces. Repeated sugar exposure creates repeated acid attacks.

Read the key points

Frequency matters as well as the total amount. Sweetened tea, juice, biscuits and frequent snacks can all contribute to repeated sugar exposure.

Practical: Keep sugary foods and drinks to mealtimes where possible, drink water between meals and use fluoride toothpaste. Attend dental reviews based on your individual risk.

MYTH: “Only sweets cause cavities.” FACT: frequent exposure to free sugars in drinks and everyday snacks contributes too.

TOOTH SENSITIVITY06

Why does cold water hurt?

Sensitivity can be linked to gum recession, enamel wear, acid erosion, cracks, cavities or recent dental treatment.

Read the key points

A brief sharp response to cold is different from spontaneous, lingering or night-time pain, which may suggest a deeper problem and needs assessment.

Practical: Use a soft brush and ask your dentist about desensitising fluoride toothpaste. Reduce frequent acidic drinks and do not brush immediately after acidic exposure. Avoid applying aspirin, clove oil or undiluted chemicals directly to teeth.

MYTH: “Sensitivity is always harmless.” FACT: it can sometimes be an early sign of decay, cracks or deeper dental disease.

RESTORATIVE DENTISTRY07

Root canal treatment and dental crowns

Root canal treatment removes inflamed or infected pulp, cleans the canal system and seals it. The tooth can remain functional after appropriate treatment and restoration.

Read the key points

A tooth may need a crown or other protective restoration depending on its type, remaining structure and fracture risk. Not every tooth needs the same restoration.

Practical: Complete the recommended permanent restoration and follow-up. Ask your dentist to explain the alternatives, expected maintenance and limitations. Antibiotics alone cannot cure an infected root canal.

MYTH: “Root canal treatment makes a tooth useless.” FACT: it can preserve a natural tooth and its function when appropriately treated and restored.

SMILE & AESTHETIC CARE08

Tooth whitening: what works and what to avoid

Professional whitening can lighten natural tooth colour, but it does not change the colour of crowns, veneers, fillings or implants. Temporary sensitivity or gum irritation can occur.

Read the key points

Suitability depends on gum health, cavities, existing restorations, pregnancy status and the cause of discolouration. Abrasive powders and acidic home remedies may wear enamel or increase sensitivity.

Practical: Have the cause of staining assessed and discuss dentist-supervised options. Avoid household bleach, lemon, charcoal powders and unregulated whitening kits.

MYTH: “Natural ingredients are always gentle.” FACT: acids and abrasives can damage teeth.

CHECK-UPS & REPLACEMENT OPTIONS09

Dental check-ups and missing teeth

There is no single check-up interval for everyone. Missing teeth can affect chewing, speech, appearance or the stability of neighbouring teeth, depending on the situation.

Read the key points

Dental review frequency depends on cavity risk, gum health, tobacco use, medical conditions and previous dental problems. A comprehensive examination may assess teeth, gums, bite and oral tissues; X-rays are selected when clinically justified.

Practical: Ask for a personalised recall plan. Options for missing teeth may include bridges, implants, removable dentures or monitoring, depending on your needs. Implants also require ongoing maintenance.

MYTH: “I only need a dentist when a tooth hurts.” FACT: early cavities and gum disease may be painless.
