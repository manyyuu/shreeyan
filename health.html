PATIENT EDUCATION · HEALTH+ NOTES

Understand your health.
One topic at a time.

Clear, practical information on common health questions. Open a topic to explore the explanation, everyday takeaways and a quick myth-versus-fact note.

METABOLIC HEALTH01

Could you have prediabetes and not know it?

Prediabetes often causes no obvious symptoms. Blood glucose may be above the healthy range without reaching the diabetes range.

Read the key points

Risk can be higher with excess abdominal weight, family history, previous gestational diabetes, hypertension, abnormal lipids or low activity. Testing may include fasting glucose and/or HbA1c, interpreted by a clinician in context.

Practical: Discuss whether testing is suitable for you and build sustainable food, activity and sleep routines. Do not use one home-glucometer reading to diagnose yourself.

MYTH: “If I feel energetic, my sugar must be normal.” FACT: early changes in blood glucose can be silent.

DIABETES CARE02

Diabetes care is more than one number

Glucose is one part of the picture. Blood pressure, cholesterol, kidney health, eye health, feet, oral health, sleep and smoking also matter.

Read the key points

HbA1c reflects glucose exposure over roughly two to three months, but it does not show every daily high or low. Anaemia, pregnancy and conditions affecting red blood cells can influence its interpretation.

Practical: Agree personal targets with your clinician, bring previous reports and review kidney function, urine albumin, eyes, feet and cardiovascular risk at appropriate intervals. Do not change medicines based only on one isolated reading.

MYTH: “A good HbA1c means every complication risk is controlled.” FACT: comprehensive risk-factor management and appropriate checks remain important.

HEART HEALTH03

High blood pressure can be silent

Blood pressure can vary with stress, activity, caffeine and measurement technique. Persistent hypertension often has no noticeable symptoms.

Read the key points

A diagnosis usually relies on repeated, properly performed readings and may include home or ambulatory monitoring. A single reading is not always enough.

Practical: Rest quietly before measuring, sit with your back supported and feet flat, use a correctly sized cuff and record repeat readings to discuss with your clinician. Do not stop prescribed tablets just because a reading improves.

MYTH: “I would feel it if my blood pressure were high.” FACT: hypertension is often silent.

CHOLESTEROL & HEART04

Cholesterol and triglycerides: why they matter

LDL particles contribute to plaque formation in blood vessels. Triglycerides can be influenced by food patterns, alcohol, diabetes, thyroid conditions, medicines and genetics.

Read the key points

Cardiovascular risk depends on the full picture, including cholesterol levels, age, diabetes, kidney health and previous heart disease. Very high triglyceride levels can increase the risk of pancreatitis.

Practical: Review your complete lipid profile with your clinician, work on sustainable activity and food habits, and take prescribed treatment consistently. Do not replace prescribed medicines with supplements without advice.

MYTH: “Triglycerides rise only after eating oily food.” FACT: excess sugar or refined starch, alcohol, insulin resistance and genetic factors can also raise them.

FOOD & NUTRITION05

Rice, roti, fruit and sugar: what should you know?

Most people with diabetes do not need to avoid every carbohydrate. Portion size, food type and the overall meal all influence blood glucose.

Read the key points

Rice or roti can fit into a balanced meal. Pairing sensible portions with dal, vegetables, curd or another protein source can help create a more balanced plate. Jaggery, honey and brown sugar still contribute sugars or rapidly available carbohydrate; whole fruit is generally preferable to fruit juice.

Practical: Choose fibre-rich foods, watch portions and read serving sizes. Personal meal guidance may be especially important for people using insulin or certain diabetes medicines.

MYTH: “Brown rice, millet, jaggery or honey cannot raise sugar.” FACT: these foods still contribute carbohydrate or sugar, so portions matter.

LIVER & METABOLIC HEALTH06

Fatty liver can occur without alcohol

Metabolic dysfunction-associated steatotic liver disease can occur alongside insulin resistance, abdominal obesity, type 2 diabetes or high triglycerides.

Read the key points

Many people have no symptoms. Blood tests alone cannot reliably exclude significant liver scarring, so assessment depends on the person's risk and may involve additional tests or imaging.

Practical: Discuss risk assessment with your clinician if you have metabolic risk factors. Gradual lifestyle changes and management of diabetes and lipids may help. Avoid relying on “liver detox” products.

MYTH: “Fatty liver is only caused by alcohol.” FACT: metabolic risk is also an important cause.

DIABETIC FOOT CARE07

Why daily foot checks matter

Nerve damage can reduce protective sensation, while poor circulation and infection can affect healing. A small blister or wound may not be felt.

Read the key points

Check the soles and between the toes daily. Wash and dry carefully, moisturise dry skin but not between the toes, and wear well-fitting footwear.

Practical: Seek prompt assessment for a wound, redness, swelling, warmth, discharge, black tissue or fever. Do not walk barefoot, cut corns yourself or apply caustic home remedies.

MYTH: “If it doesn't hurt, it cannot be serious.” FACT: nerve damage can mask pain.

KNOW THE WARNING SIGNS08

Heart attack symptoms are not always dramatic

Symptoms may include chest pressure or discomfort, breathlessness, sweating, nausea, light-headedness or unusual fatigue.

Read the key points

Discomfort may also be felt in the arm, back, jaw or upper abdomen. Some people, including women, older adults and people with diabetes, may have less typical symptoms.

Practical: If a heart attack is suspected, contact emergency medical services immediately, note when symptoms began and follow the dispatcher's advice. Do not drive yourself or wait to see whether symptoms pass.

MYTH: “A heart attack always causes crushing chest pain.” FACT: symptoms can be subtle or atypical.

PREVENTION & ACTIVITY09

Movement and health checks: make them personal

Regular activity supports heart and metabolic health. Preventive health checks work best when chosen for a person's age, history and risk—not as a fixed package.

Read the key points

For most adults, the WHO recommendation is 150–300 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic activity each week, or an equivalent amount of vigorous activity, plus muscle-strengthening activity on at least two days. Some activity is better than none.

Practical: Build activity gradually and break up long periods of sitting. During a preventive visit, discuss which checks are appropriate for your age, family history, symptoms, medicines and previous results.

MYTH: “More tests always mean better prevention.” FACT: useful screening is individualised and based on evidence.
